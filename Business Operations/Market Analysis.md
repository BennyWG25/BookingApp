These are direct competitors to our product, Booxi is the current solution in use but the others are existing and prevalent solutions in industry. YourGolfBooking is the most common booking app for driving ranges. The above table analyses the differing features between the products but they are all similar in business practise of being Software-as-a-service (SaaS). The pricing models vary significantly and often entice into full adoption of an ecosystem (such as GolfManager) through limited integrations support. 
MEWS (a hospitality booking software) is the selecting factor as it is what Mad Swans has chosen to use so must be stuck with.

An **indirect competitor** would be Excel/Google sheets. Bookings could be arranged over the phone and manually logged. This would have no direct cost but would have the indirect cost of lost business from not being able to book online, and operational costs 

This comparison shows what features other companies miss. GolfManager is likely the best software solution but it would not be very customisable to Mad Swans as they grow and develop their new sites. Having a close customer relationship with them as they expand would allow for commercial growth and a tailored solution besting anything available.

The market size, if only considering Mad Swans, has an annual sporting revenue of approximately £300k/year per site (they are rapidly expanding). This leaves significant scope for profitability for our solution, especially if incentives are aligned between each companies. If there was a cost + revenue split model then any improvements to the user experience and increasing conversion would improve profit. Common transaction fees for SaaS may be 2.5-3%. This would mean 10k/year revenue from a single site.
The wider market of golf/country club management software is $600million globally, with annual growth rate of 14.6%. With this level of growth it is easier for new entrants to compete for new customers against incumbents, particularly with low startup costs for software companies.
A report by Helcim found that administrative efficiency was the top priority for golf club managers, followed by member experience and profitability.
